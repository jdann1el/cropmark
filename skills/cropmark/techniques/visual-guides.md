# Visual guides as sketches

Use a generated visual guide when permitted imagery tools can explore a composition more usefully than a rough local layout. This is optional: real-font/code or hand-built sketches remain valid. Keep the same concept comparison and correction limits; generation does not replace the brief, source identity or visual judgment.

Specify the actual message, copy, available material and intended relationship. Preserve exact prompts and generated files as process evidence. A guide may explore typography and layout, while a final scene asset keeps required text/logos separate. Label those roles so a generated poster is not silently delivered as editable design.

Before committing to a guide, test its principal phrase with available licensed fonts in the final renderer. Compare visible width, weight, line shapes and grouping, not just nominal size. An invented display face may not be reproducible. If the actual type loses the guide's character, adjust the composition within the correction budget or disclose the loss; flat raster lettering does not satisfy editable type.

Rebuild required text, marks and composition as controllable layers; retain actual supplied subjects where identity matters. Inspect guide and reconstruction at the same feed size, then inspect the final alone. Record what survives and what changes. A good-looking guide, generated texture or successful rerender cannot establish the final's appeal or prove that this medium improves the skill.
