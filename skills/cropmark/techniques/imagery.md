# Imagery chosen for the idea

Inventory supplied photos, products, faces, authorised marks and available creation/sourcing tools before planning. Identity-critical subjects use their actual source pixels; see `photography.md`. Choose existing, sourced or generated imagery by honesty, fit, resolution and craft rather than a universal ranking. A supplied irrelevant image need not dictate an original concept, while a faithful brief may require it.

For generation, specify the subject-specific action/relation, setting, viewpoint, light/material, crop and quiet space needed for actual copy. Keep generated scene text/logos/UI out of the base; add required type/marks as controllable elements. For exact product identity, a look-alike is not a faithful photograph. Label fictional/conceptual assets in delivery notes.

For sourced assets, keep source page, creator, licence and any required credit; an image being visible on the web is not reuse permission. Do not present a found location, person or product as the client's own. Behance references teach decisions and are not an asset library.

Generate cutouts with native transparency when available. Hair, liquids, glass, chrome and smoke often work better photographed or generated on their intended background; colour keying is not semantic segmentation. Keep source, mask and processed layers separately and inspect edges on light/dark.

Plan crop and output size from the real image dimensions. If scaling reveals weak detail, use a tighter source composition, a different crop or a new permitted asset. Inspect actual material, light, perspective, anatomy, repeated objects and accidental letter-like marks at full resolution, then inspect the whole composition in feed. Use matching treatment across the set without automatically adding cinematic saturation, rim light or blur.
