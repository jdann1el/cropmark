# YouTube thumbnail

Default master: 1280×720. Inspect at full size, 320 px and around 168 px for silhouette. Keep essential content away from edges (40 px starting margin) and the lower-right duration badge (~18% wide ×15% high). A requested alternate master must be delivered as specified with its preview/QA limits declared.

The right viewer should recognise the subject and stakes quickly. Show conflict, transformation, comparison, a revealing detail, an unexpected relationship or a distinctive moment. `../techniques/story-in-frame.md` supplies mechanisms. A face, arrow, HUD, colour split, huge expression and cinematic grading are choices rather than requirements.

Use short identifying/hook words when the image cannot name the stake. Two to four words is a starting point, not a hard cap on supplied copy. Preserve the useful sentence for a sentence-led horror hook and labels for named comparisons. Image-only work is valid when it communicates enough. Set type according to the subject and reading distance; test the actual face instead of defaulting to a condensed font.

For a real face/product, use its actual source pixels when identity is required. Generated scenes/look-alikes are conceptual assets, labelled as such; they do not establish exact product features. Show only what the brief/video supports. Effects and exaggerated scale can improve communication, but do not invent success, danger, prices or performance. Make no CTR claim without actual test evidence.

Compare distinct story/composition sketches, develop the strongest, and inspect it with `../references/visual-rules.md`. Check image/type integration, believable edges, truthful implication, first read, safe zones and required copy. Deliver before/after for an upgrade without intentionally degrading the input.

Keep a short semantic headline as a coherent visible group unless the words deliberately label separate subjects. Check the relation between phrase parts as well as edge margins; spreading four words to opposite corners can weaken the transformation. Compare weight, width and silhouette at 168 px: a tidy thin headline can lose the desired impact. Use the brief/reference’s intensity rather than importing an editorial quiet style by default.

Identify the word carrying the stake before setting every line to one size. Compare an even block with a hierarchy that gives that word more width or cap height; keep the version whose small-size silhouette works with the scene. Unequal size is useful when it sharpens the promise, not a requirement for every title. Check the smaller words and accents as well as the large word, and preserve the visual clue beside the title.
